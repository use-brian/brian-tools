# Qualification frame (NFAMT)

Five checks, each answerable in one line. Run on the biggest open deals first; a big unqualified deal is the most expensive object in a pipeline.

| Check | Question | Green looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Need** | Do they have the problem, and is it urgent? | They described the pain unprompted, with a cost or deadline attached |
| **Fit** | Can we genuinely serve this well and profitably? | Matches work we have done; margin at our normal price |
| **Access** | Are we talking to the person who can say yes? | The decision maker is in the conversation, or a firm path to them exists |
| **Money** | Is the budget real at our price range? | They reacted to a price range without shock, or budget was named |
| **Timeline** | Is there a reason this happens now? | A date, an event, a consequence of waiting |

## Scoring

Each check: green / yellow / red, with the one-line evidence. No numeric scores; the point is the conversation each yellow triggers, not arithmetic.

- 4-5 green: qualified; work it with full attention.
- Reds on Access or Money: the deal is a hope, not a deal. The next action IS the missing check ("get the decision maker in the room"), nothing else.
- 3+ red: recommend disqualifying, kindly and reversibly: "not now" with a revisit date, not a burned bridge.

## Rules

- Evidence, not optimism: a check is green because of something the buyer said or did, never because the seller feels good about it.
- Qualification is continuous: a deal that was green can go red (champion left, budget froze); re-check on every stall.
- Disqualified deals get a recorded reason; the loss-reason clusters feed the outcome learning pass.

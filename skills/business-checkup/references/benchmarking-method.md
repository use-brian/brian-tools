# Benchmarking method (what to compare against, honestly)

The kit's hard rule: never invent a benchmark, an industry average, or a "typical" ratio. A made-up benchmark poisons every conclusion built on it. When a comparison would help, use this ladder, in order:

## 1. The client's own trend (default, always available)

The best benchmark for a small business is itself three months ago. Direction and rate of change are more decision-relevant than any external average: "gross margin fell from 62% to 55% in two quarters" needs no industry table to be alarming. Prefer this framing whenever history exists; building history is itself a reason to run recurring snapshots.

## 2. The client's own segments

Second-best comparisons are internal: this client vs that client, this product vs that product, this channel vs that channel. They share context (same market, same cost base) so differences are real signal.

## 3. Cited external sources (only with a citation)

If an external number genuinely matters, research it and cite it: public company filings in the same sector (for ratio shape, not absolute levels), government or statutory statistics, published industry surveys. Rules:

- Every external figure carries its source and year inline.
- State the comparability gap: a listed company's margin structure is not a 5-person agency's.
- Rate your confidence in one word (high / medium / low) next to the figure.
- If research is needed and costs anything, confirm with the user before running it.

## 4. When no benchmark exists

Say so, and fall back to arithmetic sense: "no reliable external figure; but at your prices, every 5 points of margin is HKD X per year, which pays for Y". A consequence stated in the client's own currency beats a shaky comparison.

## Anti-patterns

- "Industry standard is about 30%" with no source. Never.
- Benchmarking against the best-known company in the field ("Apple's margins are..."). Irrelevant scale.
- Using a benchmark to overrule the client's own trend. Trend first; benchmarks contextualize.

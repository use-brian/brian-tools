# Cash flow and runway

Profit is opinion; cash is fact. Run this whenever cash is mentioned, or whenever the P&L looks fine but the user sounds stressed.

## Burn and runway

- **Net burn** = cash out - cash in, per month. Use a 3-month average to smooth lumpy months; show the three values, not just the average.
- **Runway** = cash on hand / net burn. State it as months AND as a calendar date; a date creates urgency a number does not.
- Thresholds: under 6 months = flag it in every summary; under 3 months = it becomes the consult's headline regardless of what was asked.
- If the business is cash-positive, report the build rate instead and move on; do not manufacture drama.

## Working-capital timing

The gap that starves profitable businesses:

- **Receivable days**: how long clients take to pay after invoicing.
- **Payable days**: how long the business takes to pay its own bills.
- If receivable days exceed payable days, the business finances its clients by the difference. Levers: invoice on delivery (not month-end), deposits or upfront tranches, shorter terms for new clients, late fees actually enforced.

## Simple 13-week cash view

When the user can supply timing (invoices due, bills due, payroll dates), build a 13-week table: opening cash, expected in, expected out, closing cash per week. Flag the first week closing below zero or below a stated buffer. Keep it honest:

- Only include receipts with a date and a committed payer; hoped-for deals live in a separate "upside" line.
- Recompute it in snapshot runs; a 13-week view older than a month is fiction.

## Reporting discipline

Cash findings always name the driver, not just the number: "runway 4.2 months, and the driver is the receivable gap, not the cost base" changes what the user does next.

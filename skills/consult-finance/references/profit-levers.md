# Profit levers (the MECE tree and the hint catalog)

## The lever tree

Net profit = revenue (price x volume x mix) - COGS - opex. Every improvement lever lands in exactly one branch:

```
Net profit
├── Revenue
│   ├── Price      (raise rates, stop discount creep, reprice legacy clients)   -> prescribe via consult-offer
│   ├── Volume     (more leads, better conversion, more repeat)                 -> consult-growth / consult-sales
│   └── Mix        (sell more of the high-margin thing, exit low-margin work)   -> consult-strategy
├── COGS / direct  (renegotiate inputs, reduce rework, productize delivery)     -> yours
└── Opex           (cut creep, consolidate tools, fix utilization, right-size)  -> yours
```

Finance diagnoses all five branches; it prescribes only the bottom two. Routing is not deflection: state the finding and its size, then hand the decision to the owning skill.

## Hint catalog: observation -> hypothesis -> lever

| Observation | Likely hypothesis | Lever | Route |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin eroding while revenue flat or up | Pricing erosion, input cost creep, or mix shift to low-margin work | Split the three with the margin bridge; then reprice, renegotiate, or rebalance mix | offer / here / strategy |
| A cost line outgrowing revenue 2+ periods | Creep without a decision behind it | Line-by-line audit of that bucket; cancel, renegotiate, or cap | here |
| Many small tools/software lines | Subscription sprawl | Inventory, dedupe overlapping tools, annualize the keepers | here |
| People cost % rising, output flat | Utilization or scope problem | Measure billable/productive share before any staffing talk | here, then people |
| Marketing spend up, revenue flat | Acquisition cost deteriorating or channel fatigue | Cost per customer by channel; cut the worst channel first | growth |
| One client is a large revenue share at low margin | Strategic dependency mispriced | Reprice or renegotiate scope; if refused, planned exit | strategy |
| Profit fine, cash tight | Working-capital gap (receivables slower than payables) | Invoice faster, deposits, shorter terms | here |
| Revenue seasonal, costs flat | Fixed base sized for peak | Variable-ize what you can (contractors, usage pricing) | here, then ops |

## Sizing discipline

For each surviving lever, show the arithmetic in one line, annualized, in the client's currency: "cutting tools from 11% to 8% of revenue = X per year = Y points of net margin". State the one assumption the estimate leans on. Rate ease: days / weeks / months, and who has to agree. Rank by impact x ease. Present at most three; a list of ten levers is a spreadsheet, not advice.

## Verification rule

Every hint is a hypothesis until the user confirms the story behind the number. A cost line that jumped may be a deliberate investment ("we hired ahead of a contract"); challenge it as a question ("is this still earning its keep?"), never as an accusation. Levers only enter the recommendation after this pass.

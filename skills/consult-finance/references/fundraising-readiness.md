# Fundraising readiness

Two questions, answered separately and in this order. Conflating them is the classic error.

## 1. Should you raise?

Capital is a tool for removing a specific constraint. Ask:

- What is the binding constraint, and does money actually remove it? (Money fixes capacity and speed; it does not fix an offer nobody wants or a founder out of focus.)
- What does the money buy, tied to named levers from this kit's analyses? "Growth" is not a use of funds; "two delivery hires to clear the backlog that caps revenue" is.
- What is the alternative? Many small businesses are better served by the profitability path: the profit-improvement levers, priced work, deposits. Show that path beside the raise.
- Cost of the raise: dilution or debt service, plus months of founder attention. Say both.

## 2. Are you fundable?

A cold read of what a lender or investor will see:

- Clean numbers: 12+ months of consistent records, the snapshot discipline this skill builds.
- Known unit economics: contribution margin, CAC, payback (unit-economics reference); "we have not measured" reads as risk.
- Traction and direction: the trend passes; growth or improving margins, with the story for why.
- Concentration and dependency risks named honestly, with mitigations.
- A market story sized credibly. Sizing itself belongs to consult-strategy; route there if the story is the gap.

## Readiness verdict

Report as: should-raise (yes / no / not yet, with the constraint analysis), fundable (strong / workable / not yet, with the two biggest gaps), and if both are yes, what to prepare first. Never open with instrument choice (equity, debt, revenue-based); that conversation only matters after both answers are yes, and the choice rides on the runway and margin numbers already computed.

## Timing rule

Start any raise with more than 6 months of runway. Raising with less reads as distress and prices accordingly; if runway is under 6 months, the first consult is cash (cashflow-runway reference), not the deck.
